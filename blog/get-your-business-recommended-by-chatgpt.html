How to Get Your Business Recommended by ChatGPT and AI Search

More customers now ask ChatGPT and AI search who to hire. Here is how AI tools choose which businesses to recommend, and what to fix so they pick yours.

Arboreal Labs team12 November 20265 min read
Quick answer

To get your business recommended by ChatGPT and AI search, make it easy to find, understand and trust: a clear website that answers customers' questions, the same name and details on every listing, steady genuine reviews, and mentions on other trusted sites. There is no way to pay for it; AI tools recommend what they can verify.

More of your customers now skip the search results and simply ask: "Who is the best interior designer in Pune?" or "Which company can build us an inventory system?" ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI answer replies with a short list of names.

If your business is not on that list, the customer never even sees you. The good news is that getting your business recommended by ChatGPT and AI search is not a mystery or a trick. It comes down to a handful of things you can check and fix.

How do you get your business recommended by ChatGPT?

You get recommended by making your business easy for AI tools to find, understand and trust. They do not pick names at random. They read what is on the web about you and recommend the businesses that are clearest and best backed up.

There is no ad you can buy to appear in these answers. That is good news for smaller businesses: a clear, well-supported business can be named next to much bigger competitors.

Where do AI tools get their answers from?

AI tools build their answers from what they can read across the web, then summarise it. For a business, that usually means five places:

  • Your website: what you do, who it is for, where you work and the questions you answer.
  • Map and directory listings: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Justdial, IndiaMART and the directories in your industry.
  • Reviews: what customers say about you, how often and how recently.
  • Other websites that mention you: local news, industry articles, partner pages and client case studies.
  • Helpful guides: articles that answer the questions buyers ask before they choose someone.

If these sources disagree, or there is very little of them, the AI tool has nothing solid to go on and names someone else.

What makes AI recommend one business over another?

AI tools favour businesses whose details are clear, consistent and confirmed by others. In practice, that comes down to six things:

  • You say plainly what you do and where. "Commercial interior design in Pune" beats "Crafting inspired spaces".
  • Your details match everywhere. The same business name, address, phone number and services on your site and every listing.
  • Customers vouch for you. Genuine reviews that keep coming in and mention the actual service.
  • Other sites talk about you. Each trusted mention is one more place that confirms you are real and good at what you do.
  • Your pages answer real questions. How long it takes, how it works, who it suits, what to watch out for.
  • Your site can be read. It loads properly and does not block AI tools. If they cannot read it, they cannot recommend it.

Most of these are the same things that help you on Google and turn visitors into enquiries, which we cover in what makes a website bring in leads.

How do you write pages that AI tools will quote?

Write pages that answer one question each, directly, in the first sentence or two. AI tools lift short, clear answers, so make yours easy to lift:

  1. Give each service its own page. One page for each thing you sell, named the way customers would ask for it.
  2. Answer first, explain second. Start with the direct answer, then add the detail.
  3. Be specific. Name the city you serve, the types of client, the steps and the timelines.
  4. Add the questions customers ask. A short FAQ on each service page, in their words, not yours.
  5. Keep it current. Update pages when something changes, and add a new helpful guide every month or so.

Write it in your own voice. Copy that sounds like everyone else's gives AI tools no reason to pick you.

Sprout’s tip

Once a month, ask ChatGPT and Google the exact question your customers would ask, like "best CA firm in Andheri". Write down who gets named. It is the simplest way to see whether your changes are working.

How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

Most businesses see changes within a few weeks to a few months, about the same pace as Google. Fixing listings and website details can help quickly. Reviews, mentions and helpful content build up over time.

Answers also change from one question to the next, so judge progress over several months, not a single search. It is steady work, not a one-off fix.

The bottom line

ChatGPT and AI search recommend the businesses they can find, understand and trust. Say clearly what you do, keep your details the same everywhere, earn real reviews and answer your customers' questions. Do that steadily and AI tools have every reason to name you.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay ChatGPT to recommend my business?

No. There is no paid placement in ChatGPT's answers. Recommendations come from what the AI tool can find and verify about your business online.

Is AI search replacing Google?

Not entirely, but more people now start with an AI answer, and Google itself shows AI answers above its results. The good news is that the same work helps you in both.

Do I need a website to be recommended by AI?

You can be named from listings and reviews alone, but a clear website makes it far more likely. It is the one place where you control exactly what AI tools read about you.

Which AI tools should a business care about?

Start with ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and Gemini, then Perplexity and Copilot. They draw on much the same sources, so you do not need a separate plan for each.

How do I check if AI tools mention my business?

Ask them the questions your customers would ask, naming your service and your city. Repeat the same questions every month and note who is named, including your competitors.

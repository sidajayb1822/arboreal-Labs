How Long Does It Take to Build Custom Software?

Most custom business systems take 4 to 10 weeks to build. Here is what happens in each stage, what makes projects faster or slower, and how to avoid delays.

Arboreal Labs team25 October 20264 min read
Quick answer

Most custom business software, such as an inventory system, sales CRM or approval workflow, takes 4 to 10 weeks to build. That covers about a week of discovery, a week of planning, 2 to 6 weeks of building with weekly demos, and a few days to launch and train your team.

It is usually the first question business owners ask, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you are building. But "it depends" is not very useful when you are planning a launch, so here is a clearer picture.

For a focused business system, such as an inventory tool, a sales CRM or an approval workflow, most projects take 4 to 10 weeks from the first conversation to your team using it every day.

What happens in those weeks

A well-run project moves through five stages. Knowing them helps you see where time actually goes.

Discovery (about 1 week)

The team learns exactly how your business works today: who does what, where information comes from, and what keeps going wrong. This is the most important week of the project. Skipping it is the most common reason software ends up not fitting.

Planning (about 1 week)

You get a complete map of what will be built, screen by screen, before any building starts. This is where you catch misunderstandings while they are still cheap to fix.

Building (2 to 6 weeks)

The software is built in short cycles, and you see working progress every week. Weekly demos matter: they let you steer while there is still time, so nothing at launch comes as a surprise.

Launch (a few days)

The system goes live, existing data is moved across, and your team is trained on how to use it.

Support (ongoing)

After launch, the software keeps improving as your business changes. Good software is never really "finished"; it grows with you.

What makes a project faster

  • A clear owner on your side. One person who can answer questions and make decisions quickly can save weeks.
  • A focused first version. Building the core of what you need first, then adding to it, is faster and safer than trying to do everything at once.
  • Quick feedback on demos. The sooner you react to what you see each week, the sooner the next step can start.
  • Clean starting data. If your current records are tidy, moving them across is quick.
Sprout’s tip

Put the weekly demo in everyone's calendar on day one. Projects rarely slip because of the building. They slip while feedback waits.

What makes a project slower

  • Changing the goal halfway through. New ideas are welcome, but big changes mid-build reset part of the work. Keep a list and add them in the next round.
  • Too many decision-makers. When every screen needs five approvals, progress stalls.
  • Connecting to many outside tools. Each connection to another product adds time, especially if that product is old or poorly documented.
  • Unclear processes. If the team does not agree on how something should work today, the software cannot settle it for them.

Why some projects take months or years

You may have heard of software projects that drag on for a year or more. That usually happens when a project tries to replace everything at once, when the requirements are written up front and never checked with real users, or when nobody sees working software until the very end.

Short, focused builds with weekly check-ins avoid most of these problems.

How to plan your own timeline

  1. Decide what must be true on launch day, and keep that list short.
  2. Book time with the people who will use the system, not only the managers.
  3. Gather your current spreadsheets and documents before discovery starts.
  4. Agree on who signs off each stage, and how quickly.

Do those four things, and a 4 to 10 week timeline is very realistic for most business systems.

The short answer

Most custom business software can be ready in 4 to 10 weeks. Projects run on time when the scope is focused, decisions are quick, and you see progress every week. If someone quotes you a much longer timeline for a single business system, ask them what is taking the time.

Frequently asked questions

Can custom software be built in less than a month?

A small, very focused tool can be. Most business systems that replace several spreadsheets or tools take 4 to 10 weeks so they are planned properly and tested with real users.

Why do some software projects take a year or more?

Usually because they try to replace everything at once, the plan is never checked with real users, or nobody sees working software until the end. Short builds with weekly demos avoid this.

What happens after the software launches?

Your team is trained, and the software keeps being updated and improved as your business changes. We stay on to support and grow it as a managed service.
